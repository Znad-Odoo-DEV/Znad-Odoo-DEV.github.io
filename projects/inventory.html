Inventory restructure: one warehouse per site store

Site stores turned into warehouses of their own, so forecast, incoming stock and replenishment answer per project, and the stores’ movements priced into journal entries for the accountant.

Private client repository

  • 0quantities changed
  • 28draft valuation entries
  • 1,298lines flagged for a cost

The problem

When the Studio store app moved into Inventory, each site store became a location inside the one warehouse of its company. Odoo answers forecast, incoming stock, a purchase order’s Deliver To and replenishment per warehouse, so the forecast for cement showed 149 units on hand and no way to tell which site they were at.

What I built

One warehouse per site store. Odoo 19 always gives a new warehouse a fresh stock location and refuses a replenishment location beside another, so the tool creates the warehouse normally, re-parents the store’s existing location under it as its stock, repoints every operation type, route and reorder rule that named the new empty one, and removes it. No stock move or quantity is touched; cost stays per company, and project cost still comes from the analytic account on consumption.

The dry run performs the whole conversion, compares a snapshot of every store, product and lot before and after, and rolls back. It was proven on a local copy first: a purchase received on its site, a reorder rule buying for its own site’s quantity, consumption and a site-to-site transfer, and a second run that changed nothing. Two old transfers still reserving stock in an empty store were cancelled first, so it could be archived.

Stock valuation for the books

The stores also had to reach the accounts. Every company values stock periodically and almost no product had a standard cost, so a read-only report first showed what each movement would post: receipts to site inventory against goods received not invoiced, consumption to the category’s expense with the project’s analytic account, no entry for a transfer between sites, and stock moving between companies through a transit account.

Cost is the last confirmed purchase price, then the standard price; a line with neither is flagged, never posted at zero. The choice was draft entries in Odoo for the accountant to review: 28 of them, one per company and month, in an Inventory Valuation journal, with the open questions written down beside them.

The result

Live on production: 13 warehouses, 5 empty stores archived, and not one quantity or movement changed (on staging, 1,897 quantity lines and 10,298 movement lines were compared before and after). Forecast, incoming and replenishment now answer per site.

8,901 store movements priced into 28 draft journal entries, with 1,298 stock lines flagged as having no cost for purchasing to price.
