Internal requests & approvals

The problem

Requests lived in a Studio app: one model with ten line models, 27 automations and 50 server actions. Five of its eight print reports produced a blank page, and the only link from a purchase order, a bill or a payment back to the request that asked for it ran through that app, so deleting it would have cut the trail.

What I built

One request model for what the sites and the office actually ask for (leave, salary advance, material, payment), each with its own approval route and its own record.

The second module is the half that usually goes missing: what an approved request produces. An approved leave, advance or resignation becomes the payroll document it implies, so the approval and its consequence cannot drift apart. Granted but never paid, or paid but never granted, is a state the data will not hold.

A purchase bridge writes the request onto the purchase order, the vendor bill and the payment, filled at install from the old chain. Three of Studio’s eight reports were rebuilt; the five that printed a blank page were not carried. Approvers’ signatures moved onto their user records.

The result

Live on production with 1,150 requests carried. 578 of 582 purchase orders, 189 of 191 bills and 136 of 137 payments still trace back to their request; the few that do not never named one in Studio.

430 automated checks across 14 suites. Then the Studio app was deleted on production: 16 models, about 44,000 rows, 27 automations, 50 server actions and 12,182 chatter messages.
