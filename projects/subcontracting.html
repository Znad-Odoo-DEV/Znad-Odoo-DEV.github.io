Subcontracting & payment certificates

The money going out: a subcontract carries its own bill of quantities beside ours, and payment certificates set what is claimed against what was agreed and measured.

The problem

Subcontract certificates lived in Studio, and their totals were stored rather than computed: the stored total summed a field that was no longer on the line model, so 40 of 206 certificates no longer matched their own lines. Agreements were matched to their bills by sharing a contractor and a project, and signed contracts sat inline on the record.

What I built

A subcontract carries its own bill of quantities beside ours, so what an item was sold for sits next to what is being paid for it, and the margin is a fact on the record rather than a calculation somebody does later. Call-offs raised against the agreement become LPOs.

Payment certificates close the loop: what a subcontractor claims, against what was agreed and what the site actually measured. A claim that outruns the measurement is visible before it is approved, not after it is paid.

Contractor agreements became blanket orders in standard Purchase Agreements, named by their subcontract reference and carrying project, subcontract and scope, with a button from each agreement to its certificates.

The result

Reconciled against the live figure, quantity times rate, never against the stored column: 203 of 206 certificates agree, and the other three differ by under 0.30.

64 agreements carried; 57 purchase orders, 181 certificates and 177 vendor bills linked back to them; 56 signed contracts filed on their agreement’s chatter.
